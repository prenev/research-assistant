Short plain-language definitions of terms used on this site. Add your own as you meet new ones.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **FTD** | Frontotemporal dementia. A group of conditions caused by loss of nerve cells in the frontal and temporal parts of the brain. |
| **Incident** | A new diagnosis that appears during follow-up, as opposed to someone who already had it at the start. |
| **Biomarker** | A measurable signal in the body (here, a protein in blood) that tracks a condition or predicts it. |
| **NfL** | Neurofilament light chain. A protein released when nerve cells are damaged. A widely used blood marker of that damage. |
| **GFAP** | Glial fibrillary acidic protein. A protein found mainly in star-shaped support cells of the brain (astrocytes). |
| **CRP** | C-reactive protein. A general marker of inflammation in the body. |
| **Cytokine** | A small signalling protein that helps control inflammation and immune responses. |
| **Olink** | A laboratory platform that measures many proteins from a small blood sample. |
| **NEFL** | The name the Olink platform uses for NfL. |
| **UK Biobank** | A large UK research cohort with health records and stored blood samples from about half a million volunteers. |
| **Cox regression** | A statistical model for how long until an event happens, such as a diagnosis, taking follow-up time into account. |
| **Penalised regression** | A way of fitting a model that discourages it from relying on too many predictors, which helps when there are many candidates. |
| **Cross-validation** | Testing a model on data it was not trained on, by repeatedly splitting the data. *Nested* adds a second layer so that choosing settings does not leak into the test. |
| **AUC** | Area under the curve. A score from 0.5 (no better than chance) to 1 (perfect) for how well a model separates people who develop the condition from those who do not. |
| **Calibration** | Whether predicted risks match the risks actually observed. |
| **Prespecified** | Decided and written down before looking at the outcome data, so the analysis cannot be steered by the results. |
| **Sensitivity analysis** | Repeating the analysis under different assumptions to see whether the conclusion holds. |
